Where the money is — the 10-spot paytable

The paytable is U-shaped: matching zero pays $5, and matching 5+ pays. Matching 1–4 pays nothing, and that dead zone swallows 89% of all draws. The single biggest source of expected value is the 0-match payout at 36%.

Generator sweep — searching for the algorithmstopped

A background process runs continuously, generating draws from known random-number generators and comparing them to real draws in true machine order. A match on order would be conclusive: one ordered draw carries 122.7 bits, so a hit could not be coincidence.

mean is the average number of balls matched out of 20. Chance gives exactly 5.0 (20×20÷80) — every row sits there. P(coincidence) is the probability that this many random tries would produce a result at least as good as that row’s best, computed from the hypergeometric distribution as 1−(1−p)N. Values near 100% mean “entirely expected”; anything under 5% would be worth investigating.

Draw order — which ball comes out when

The archive publishes every draw sorted, which throws away the sequence the machine actually produced. This table is built from the true pull order, collected separately and still filling in. Each position gets 8,179 samples spread over 80 balls, so chance alone puts 102.2 in every cell (sd 10.05) — and the biggest of 80 such cells lands near z = +2.5 without anything being wrong. Read the z column, not the counts.
